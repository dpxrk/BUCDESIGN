# 06 · Voice & tone

> Italian-articulate, quiet, declarative. The Maison's status is assumed,
> not asserted. No exclamation marks. No "world-class." No shouting.

## Voice attributes

- **Italian-articulate** — complete sentences, subordinate clauses, rhythm.
  Write as you would speak in a serious room.
- **Quiet** — never exclamation marks, never ALL CAPS for emphasis (use
  small caps), never bold within body text.
- **Specific** — prefer named places, dates, craftsmen, and techniques
  over abstractions. *"The workshop on Via degli Orefici"* rather than
  *"our atelier."* *"Macri Giglio"* rather than *"a heritage collection."*
- **Heritage-anchored, not nostalgic** — the past is always in service of
  a present argument.
- **Confident, not boastful** — no "world-class," "best-in-class,"
  "leading," "premium." The Maison's status is assumed, not asserted.

## Vocabulary preferences

| Preferred                  | Avoid                                  |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Maison                     | brand, company                         |
| Atelier · workshop         | factory                                |
| Boutique                   | store                                  |
| Creation · piece           | product                                |
| Client                     | customer · consumer                    |
| High Jewellery             | High Jewelry (American spelling)       |
| Savoir-faire               | expertise · capability                 |
| Hand-engraved              | engraved                               |
| Macri · Tulle · Rombi · Eternelle | "our heritage collections" (generic) |

The British spelling **"Jewellery"** is the Maison's own preferred form in
corporate communications. Keep this consistent across decks.

## Slide titling

- **Sentence case**, never Title Case
- **Declarative**, not interrogative — *"Macri remains the foundation of
  the wholesale ladder"* rather than *"What about Macri?"*
- **One line, ideally** — maximum two
- **Never colon-led** — *"Strategy: A path forward"* is forbidden. Write
  a single declarative line.

### Good titles

- *Macri remains the foundation of the wholesale ladder.*
- *Two truths, one decision.*
- *The Americas now lead.*
- *A century of openings.*
- *At the bench, and behind the bench.*

### Bad titles

- ~~Our Strategy For The Future~~ (Title Case, abstract, no claim)
- ~~Q4 Results: A Strong Quarter!~~ (colon-led, exclamation, boastful)
- ~~LEADING THE INDUSTRY~~ (ALL CAPS, status-asserting)
- ~~What is our plan?~~ (interrogative — say the answer instead)

## Numerical conventions

- Currency in **€** by default (Italian Maison; Richemont reports in EUR)
- One decimal place maximum for percentages — *"12.4%"*, not *"12.43%"*
- "M" for millions, "Bn" for billions (consistent with Richemont investor
  decks)
- Years as four digits — *"2026"*, not *"'26"*
- Use the non-breaking thin space between figure and unit where possible
- Tabular figures in Georgia for any data table or KPI block

## Dates

| Context                  | Format                          |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Footer                   | `05.2026`                       |
| Body copy                | *May 2026* (English), *Maggio 2026* (Italian) |
| Timeline ticks           | `1919`                          |
| Section breaks           | *Q1 FY26* or *first half FY26*  |

## Italian phrases — when, and when not

The system supports Italian phrasing at brand-essence moments (cover,
section dividers, closing). Don't sprinkle it through body copy.

- **Welcome on the cover** — none; lead with the deck title in English.
- **Closing** — *"Grazie."* is the default. *"Mille grazie."* on a deck
  delivered to the Chairman personally.
- **Atelier copy** — *"Fatto a mano"* and *"a Milano"* are permitted as
  flourishes in image captions, not in slide titles.

## When in doubt

Write less. The Maison is quieter than its content. A confident sentence
on a page of generous whitespace is more Buccellati than three competing
ones.
